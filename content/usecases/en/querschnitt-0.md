Prepare agendas and questions for supervision and summarise agreed actions afterwards from your own notes.

## Problem

Supervision time is short: without an agenda key decisions slip, expectations stay implicit, and agreed actions sit in scattered notes that nobody writes up.

## Result

A timed agenda with the decisions you need and an action list with owners and deadlines checked against your notes – better-used meetings and a shared written record.

## Steps

1. Paste your progress notes and open questions
2. Ask for a timed agenda and three decisions
3. After the meeting, turn your notes into SMART actions
4. Check each action against your notes
5. Share the action list with your supervisors

## Concepts

### Lee's approaches to doctoral supervision

Lee (2008) distinguishes five approaches: functional (project management), enculturation (joining the discipline), critical thinking, emancipation (personal development) and relationship development. Naming the approach an agenda item needs clarifies what you ask your supervisor for.

### SMART actions

Doran's (1981) criteria for objectives: specific, measurable, assignable (now often 'achievable'), realistic and time-related. An action with a named owner and a deadline can be checked at the next meeting.

### Decision and action log

A running record that separates decisions (what was agreed and why), actions (who does what by when) and open questions. It makes progress traceable for annual reviews and protects both you and your supervisors.

## References

- Lee, A. (2008). How are doctoral students supervised? Concepts of doctoral research supervision. Studies in Higher Education, 33(3), 267–281. doi:10.1080/03075070802049202
- Doran, G. T. (1981). There's a S.M.A.R.T. way to write management's goals and objectives. Management Review, 70(11), 35–36.

## Own material

### Bring

Your own short notes on progress since the last supervision meeting and a few open questions; no personal details about other people.

### Steps

1. Paste your notes and ask for a 45-minute meeting agenda.
2. Ask for three decisions you need and questions to ask.
3. After a meeting, paste your own notes and ask for an action list.
4. Check the action list against your notes line by line.

### Prompt

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] preparing for a [45-minute] supervision meeting. My notes on progress and open issues are: [paste notes]. Draft an agenda with time slots, the three decisions I need from my supervisors, and specific questions for each agenda item. After the meeting I will paste my notes; then summarise them as a table: action, owner, deadline, and add any open questions. Use only what is in my notes and do not add actions or agreements that are not there.
```

### Check

Every action in the summary appears in your notes, and nothing you agreed in the meeting is missing.

### Limit

The AI assistant does not join or record the meeting; it works only from the notes you write and paste.

## Technology

Paste your own notes into your AI assistant. A transcription tool such as Otter.ai can be connected only if your university permits it, and recording needs every participant's consent.

## Levels

### Chat

Run the starter prompt with your notes pasted before the meeting. Afterwards, in the same chat, paste your meeting notes, ask for the action table, and ask which agenda items still have no agreed action.

### Project / Notebook

Create one project or notebook for supervision and add your project plan, earlier action lists and your own meeting notes (no personal details about others) as references. Before each meeting ask which actions are still open and what belongs on the agenda – you gain a running record across meetings that a single chat forgets.

### Custom assistant

Instructions: agenda with time slots, the three decisions you need, questions per item, actions as a table with owner and deadline in SMART form, use only the pasted notes, never add agreements that are not there, list open questions. No Knowledge needed. Starter prompts: "Agenda for my 45-minute meeting" and "Action list from these notes". Reusable for every supervision meeting.
