Check that every reference exists and its details are correct – and that each cited source really supports the sentence it is cited for.

## Problem

Wrong reference details and quotation errors – sources that do not say what they are cited for – slip in easily, especially with AI-suggested sources, and damage credibility.

## Result

A per-reference check against DOIs and publisher records, and a support rating (supports, partly supports, does not) for each citing sentence with quoted evidence – you open every flagged source and decide.

## Steps

1. Paste the paragraph with its reference list
2. Ask for a DOI and metadata check per reference
3. Ask whether each source supports its sentence, quoting it
4. Open every flagged source and judge yourself

## Concepts

### Quotation (citation) accuracy

Whether a cited source actually supports the statement it is cited for. Jergas and Baethge's meta-analysis found quotation errors common in medical journals, a substantial share of them major – the same check applies in every field.

### Reference fabrication by language models

Language models can produce plausible but non-existent references or real ones with wrong details. Walters and Wilder found both problems in ChatGPT-generated bibliographies, so every AI-suggested reference needs an existence and metadata check.

### DOI and Crossref metadata

A Digital Object Identifier resolves to the publisher's record; Crossref metadata confirm authors, year, title, journal, volume and pages. Resolving the DOI is the first existence check, comparing metadata the second.

### Citation statement classification

scite classifies citing statements as supporting, contrasting or mentioning (Nicholson et al. 2021). Use the same logic for your rating: supports, partly supports, does not support – always with the quoted passage.

## Practice exercise

### Task

Check the first 15 references of the Christidis et al. (2023) paper and one claim it cites to the 2020 UK study. You learn whether the assistant verifies metadata properly, notices flawed details in a published list, and judges citation support with real quotes.

### Prompt

```text
Attached are two PDFs: Christidis et al. (2023) on 50 °C in the Mediterranean and Christidis et al. (2020) on 30 to 40 °C in the UK. 1) For references 1 to 15 of the 2023 paper, search for each and give a working DOI or link; say 'not found' if you cannot find it, and flag any difference in authors, year, title, journal, volume or pages. 2) In the 2023 introduction, the sentence beginning 'An attribution study indeed confirmed' cites reference 15. Using the attached 2020 PDF, say whether that source supports the sentence, partly supports it, or does not, and quote the passage you rely on. Do not guess; if you cannot see a source, say so.
```

### Check

Check the page ranges of references 4 and 10 against the publishers' records yourself – they deserve a close look – and see whether the assistant flags them. For reference 15, read the 2020 abstract on 40 °C return times.

## Own material

### Bring

A paragraph of your own writing with five to eight citations and the matching reference list entries.

### Steps

1. Paste the paragraph and its references.
2. With web search switched on (if your tool offers it), ask the assistant to check that each reference exists, with a link or DOI.
3. Ask whether each source supports the sentence it is cited for.
4. Open each flagged source and decide yourself.

### Prompt

```text
I am checking the references in a paragraph of my [thesis or paper]. For each reference below: 1) search for it and give a working link or DOI; say 'not found' if you cannot find it, and flag differences in authors, year, title or journal. 2) For each sentence with a citation, say whether the cited source – as far as you can see from its abstract or full text – supports the claim, partly supports it, or does not, and quote the passage you rely on. Do not guess; if you cannot see enough of a source, say so.

Paragraph: [paste]
References: [paste]
```

### Check

Each 'found' reference opens the right paper, and for two sentences you read the source yourself to confirm the assistant's judgement.

### Limit

The assistant searches the open web and may not see paywalled full texts; a connected database (for example OpenAlex, which can check whether references exist, or scite for citation statements) makes this more reliable.

## Technology

Many assistants check references by web search, depending on your tool and settings; with OpenAlex or scite connected through a low-code platform or your own code (check with your university first) it matches metadata and citation statements directly. The Zotero MCP server runs locally – with a coding assistant in your editor or a desktop assistant that supports MCP, not in a plain browser chat.

## Levels

### Chat

Run the starter prompt with web search switched on (depending on your tool and settings) and both PDFs attached. Then, in the same chat, ask the assistant to show the link it used for every reference it marked as matching.

### Project / Notebook

Add the papers you cite as PDFs (for the practice, C3 and B2) and your draft paragraph. Ask, sentence by sentence, whether the cited source supports the claim, with a quote. Depending on your tool, the project or notebook answers only from its references, so a missing source shows up as a gap instead of a vague web summary.

### Custom assistant

Create a configured assistant, for example "Reference checker" (e.g. a Copilot agent, a ChatGPT project, a Gemini Gem or a Claude project). In the standing instructions, set the two-step check: first existence and metadata (working DOI or link, "not found" if absent, flag any difference in authors, year, title, journal, volume or pages), then support (supports, partly, does not) with a quoted passage; never guess. Keep web search on if your tool offers it. Starter prompt: "Check these references and the claims they are cited for." Reusable for every chapter before submission.

### Automation / own code

Connect OpenAlex to check that references exist and match their metadata, and scite to see how a source is cited elsewhere – both as MCP servers. A configured assistant without connections cannot reach external databases, and web search misses paywalled records. On GitHub: a script checks every DOI in your reference list against the Crossref or OpenAlex API and flags metadata that does not match. Check data protection and approval with your university first.
