Draft items, interview guides and response scales; check for clarity and for double-barrelled or leading questions, and map every item to your research questions – also across survey and interview in mixed methods.

## Problem

Draft items often hide double-barrelled, leading or ambiguous wording, and some research questions or facets of the construct end up with no item – usually noticed only after data collection.

## Result

Revised items with each problem named, an item-to-construct matrix that exposes gaps, and a response-scale proposal you then check against validated instruments and expert relevance ratings.

## Steps

1. Define the construct, its facets, target group and research questions
2. Paste draft items and ask for a problem table
3. Ask for revised wording and an item-to-construct matrix
4. Search published sources for validated scales before writing new items
5. Ask a few experts to rate each item's relevance

## Concepts

### Construct definition and content validity

Define the construct and its facets before writing items. Content validity is the degree to which items cover every facet and nothing else; an item-to-construct (blueprint) matrix shows gaps and over-represented facets, in surveys and interview guides alike.

### Item-writing rules

Avoid double-barrelled items (two questions in one), leading or loaded wording, double negatives, vague quantifiers such as 'often', jargon and unwarranted assumptions. Each item asks one thing, in the respondents' language, and fits its response scale.

### Four-stage response model (Tourangeau)

Answering involves comprehension, retrieval, judgement and response mapping. Diagnose each faulty item by stage: unclear terms fail comprehension, long recall periods fail retrieval, mismatched options fail response mapping.

### Likert-type rating scales

Ordered agreement or frequency categories, commonly five or seven points, with consistent direction and labelled endpoints or all points labelled. Decide deliberately on a midpoint and a 'don't know' option; reliable scale scores need several items per facet.

### Content validity index (CVI)

Experts rate each item's relevance on a four-point scale; the item-level CVI (I-CVI) is the share rating 3 or 4. Lynn (1986) set the minimum agreement by panel size; Polit, Beck and Owen (2007) recommend an I-CVI of at least 0.78.

## Own material

### Bring

Five to eight draft survey items or interview questions of your own, on a non-sensitive topic.

### Steps

1. Paste your draft items and describe the construct and target group.
2. Ask the assistant to flag double-barrelled, leading, ambiguous or jargon-heavy items.
3. Ask for revised wording and a suitable response scale.
4. Check that each revision still measures your intended construct and every research question is covered.

### Prompt

```text
I am developing a [survey or interview guide] on [construct] for [target group]. Review the draft items below. Show a table: item number, original wording, problem (double-barrelled, leading, ambiguous, jargon, mismatch with response scale, or none), revised wording. Keep the meaning and the construct unchanged. Add a column mapping each item to the research question or construct it serves, and flag research questions that no item covers. Then suggest a suitable response scale and say whether a validated scale for this construct might already exist; if you are not sure, say so; do not invent sources.

[paste items]
```

### Check

Each flagged problem is real when you re-read the original item, and each revised item still asks about the construct you intended.

## Technology

Paste the items into your AI assistant (e.g. Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini), or upload your draft questionnaire or interview guide as a Word file; ask for the review as a table to copy into Word or Excel. Check suggested validated scales in the published sources yourself.

## Levels

### Chat

Run the starter prompt with your draft items. In the same chat, paste your revised items and ask for the same check and construct map again, so you see whether the fixes created new problems or gaps.

### Project / Notebook

Collect your research questions, construct definitions, questionnaire and interview guide drafts, and papers that report validated scales for your constructs. Ask which construct or research question no item covers, across survey and interview. The notebook keeps every version, so the item-to-construct matrix stays consistent as the instrument grows.

### Custom assistant

Call it 'Item reviewer'. Instructions: the problem types (double-barrelled, leading, ambiguous, jargon, scale mismatch), the table format, 'keep meaning and construct unchanged' and 'never invent validated scales or sources'. Knowledge: your construct definitions and research questions. Starter prompts: 'Review these items: [paste]' and 'Map these items to my constructs'. Reusable for every new draft and shareable with co-authors.
