Check whether a systematic, scoping or narrative review fits your question, the literature and your time – and draft the protocol, including testable inclusion and exclusion criteria and borderline cases.

## Problem

It is tempting to call a review 'systematic' without the time or literature to support it; vague inclusion criteria then fail halfway through screening.

## Result

A reasoned choice of review type for your question and deadline, and a protocol outline along PRISMA-P with testable criteria and borderline cases – ready to discuss with your supervisor.

## Steps

1. State your question, likely literature volume and deadline
2. Ask your AI assistant to compare systematic, scoping, narrative and rapid reviews
3. Draft the protocol with criteria and borderline examples
4. Pilot the criteria on five abstracts you know
5. Decide on registration (PROSPERO or OSF) with your supervisor

## Concepts

### Review typology (Grant & Booth)

Fourteen review types compared by search, appraisal, synthesis and analysis (the SALSA framework). Systematic reviews answer focused questions with exhaustive searches and appraisal; scoping reviews map evidence; narrative reviews are selective.

### Systematic vs scoping review (Munn et al.)

Choose a scoping review to identify the types of evidence, clarify concepts, map gaps or prepare a systematic review; choose a systematic review to answer a specific question, for example on effectiveness or meaning.

### Scoping framework (Arksey & O'Malley)

Five stages: identify the question, identify relevant studies, select studies, chart the data, collate and report; an optional sixth stage is consultation. Report scoping reviews with PRISMA-ScR.

### PRISMA-P

A 17-item checklist for reporting systematic review protocols: rationale, eligibility criteria, sources, search strategy, selection and extraction, risk of bias, synthesis. Registering the protocol on PROSPERO (health-related outcomes) or OSF (any field) fixes the plan in advance.

### Rapid review

A streamlined systematic approach – for example fewer databases, or single screening with verification – for decisions under time pressure. Report every shortcut as a limitation.

## Practice exercise

### Task

Choose a review type for a practice question on European heat and drought, draft eligibility criteria, then pilot them on five practice abstracts. You learn whether the assistant's criteria can be judged from an abstract or leave many records 'unsure'.

### Prompt

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher planning a literature review. My research question: 'What does the published evidence say about how human influence has changed the likelihood of heat and drought extremes in Europe?' Expected amount of literature: moderate. Time available: three months. Compare a systematic, scoping, narrative and rapid review for my case in a table: purpose, what my question would need, effort, risks. Recommend one type and name the conditions under which you would recommend another. Then draft inclusion and exclusion criteria derived from my question only, in a table: criterion, include or exclude, stage judged (title and abstract or full text), example of a borderline case. Finally, apply the criteria to records B2, B3, C1, C3 and C5 in the attached abstracts file, using title and abstract only, and say which criteria were hard to judge.
```

### Check

Read the five abstracts yourself: Christidis et al. (2023) covers the Middle East as well as the Mediterranean, and Perkins-Kirkpatrick and Lewis (2020) reports trends without attribution – both should trigger a criterion. Compare the outline with PRISMA-P.

## Own material

### Bring

Your research question, a rough idea of how much literature exists, and your deadline for the review.

### Steps

1. Paste your research question, the expected amount of literature and your deadline.
2. Ask your AI assistant to compare systematic, scoping, narrative and rapid reviews for your case.
3. Ask for a protocol outline with testable inclusion and exclusion criteria and borderline cases.
4. Apply the criteria to five abstracts you know and compare the outline with your field's reporting guideline.

### Prompt

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] planning a literature review. My research question: [question]. Expected amount of literature: [few / moderate / large, if known]. Time available: [weeks or months]. Compare a systematic review, a scoping review, a narrative review and a rapid review for my case in a table: purpose, what my question would need, effort, and risks. Recommend one type and name the conditions under which you would recommend another. Then draft a protocol outline: review question, its elements (for example PICOC), sources and databases, time span and languages, inclusion and exclusion criteria, screening stages, data extraction and synthesis approach, and whether registering the protocol (for example on PROSPERO or OSF) is usual in my field. For the inclusion and exclusion criteria, derive them from the research question only, show a table (criterion, include or exclude, judged at title-and-abstract or full-text stage, example of a borderline case) and say which criteria are too open, too strict or hard to judge from an abstract. Mark anything you are unsure about.
```

### Check

The recommendation fits your question and your time, the criteria give the decisions you expect for five abstracts you know, and the outline covers the items of your field's reporting guideline (for example PRISMA-P or the JBI guidance for scoping reviews).

### Limit

Choosing the review type is a methodological decision for you and your supervisors; the assistant's outline is a starting point, not a protocol.

## Technology

A plain chat with the starter prompt; ask for the protocol as an outline you can paste into Word. For practice, attach the abstracts file. The deep-research and literature-review skills for coding assistants such as GitHub Copilot offer structured modes for systematic and scoping reviews.

## Levels

### Chat

Attach the abstracts file (AB) and run the starter prompt. In the same chat, ask the assistant to tighten every criterion that left a record 'unsure' and to apply the revised criteria to the same five records.

### Project / Notebook

Add your question, PRISMA-P (or the JBI guidance for scoping reviews), your library's guide on review types and the pilot abstracts (AB). Ask which review type fits and which PRISMA-P items your draft protocol misses. You come back as the protocol matures, with each version and your supervisor's comments in one place.

### Custom assistant

Call it 'Review planner'. Instructions: compare systematic, scoping, narrative and rapid reviews for the given question and time, recommend one with conditions, draft criteria from the question only with stage and borderline case, flag criteria hard to judge from an abstract, and mark uncertainty. Knowledge: PRISMA-P and your field's guidance. Starter prompt: 'Which review type fits this question and deadline?' Reusable for every new review.
