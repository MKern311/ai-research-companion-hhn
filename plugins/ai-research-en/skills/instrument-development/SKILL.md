# Instrument development

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/design-1

## Goal

Draft items, interview guides and response scales; check for clarity and for double-barrelled or leading questions, and map every item to your research questions – also across survey and interview in mixed methods.

Revised items with each problem named, an item-to-construct matrix that exposes gaps, and a response-scale proposal you then check against validated instruments and expert relevance ratings.

## When to use

Draft items often hide double-barrelled, leading or ambiguous wording, and some research questions or facets of the construct end up with no item – usually noticed only after data collection.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste your draft items and describe the construct and target group.
2. Ask the assistant to flag double-barrelled, leading, ambiguous or jargon-heavy items.
3. Ask for revised wording and a suitable response scale.
4. Check that each revision still measures your intended construct and every research question is covered.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am developing a [survey or interview guide] on [construct] for [target group]. Review the draft items below. Show a table: item number, original wording, problem (double-barrelled, leading, ambiguous, jargon, mismatch with response scale, or none), revised wording. Keep the meaning and the construct unchanged. Add a column mapping each item to the research question or construct it serves, and flag research questions that no item covers. Then suggest a suitable response scale and say whether a validated scale for this construct might already exist; if you are not sure, say so; do not invent sources.

[paste items]
```

## Quality check

Each flagged problem is real when you re-read the original item, and each revised item still asks about the construct you intended.

## Limits

- Risk: Your own unpublished drafts, reviews or third-party content.

## Key concepts

- **Construct definition and content validity** – Define the construct and its facets before writing items. Content validity is the degree to which items cover every facet and nothing else; an item-to-construct (blueprint) matrix shows gaps and over-represented facets, in surveys and interview guides alike.
- **Item-writing rules** – Avoid double-barrelled items (two questions in one), leading or loaded wording, double negatives, vague quantifiers such as 'often', jargon and unwarranted assumptions. Each item asks one thing, in the respondents' language, and fits its response scale.
- **Four-stage response model (Tourangeau)** – Answering involves comprehension, retrieval, judgement and response mapping. Diagnose each faulty item by stage: unclear terms fail comprehension, long recall periods fail retrieval, mismatched options fail response mapping.
- **Likert-type rating scales** – Ordered agreement or frequency categories, commonly five or seven points, with consistent direction and labelled endpoints or all points labelled. Decide deliberately on a midpoint and a 'don't know' option; reliable scale scores need several items per facet.
- **Content validity index (CVI)** – Experts rate each item's relevance on a four-point scale; the item-level CVI (I-CVI) is the share rating 3 or 4. Lynn (1986) set the minimum agreement by panel size; Polit, Beck and Owen (2007) recommend an I-CVI of at least 0.78.
