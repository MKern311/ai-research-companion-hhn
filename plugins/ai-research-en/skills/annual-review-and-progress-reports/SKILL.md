# Annual review & progress reports

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/querschnitt-1

## Goal

Turn lab notebooks and notes into monthly progress reports for your supervisor, and structure progress, plans and risks for annual, transfer or confirmation reviews.

A draft under your form's headings with gaps marked [TO ADD], a risk register and evidence of skills development – a starting point you complete with verified facts.

## When to use

Monthly reports to your supervisor and annual or transfer reviews arrive at busy times; reconstructing progress from lab notebooks and scattered notes and fitting it to the required headings takes days.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste the section headings of your monthly report or review form.
2. Paste your notes and ask for a draft under those headings.
3. Ask for a risk table with likelihood, impact and mitigation.
4. Check every achievement and date against your notes and records.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] preparing my [monthly progress report for my supervisor / annual / transfer / confirmation review]. The report needs these sections: [paste headings]. My notes are: [paste notes]. Draft the report under these headings in a factual, concise style of about [word count] words. Include progress against the objectives of the last period, outputs, training, a plan for the next period (month or year), and a table of risks with likelihood, impact and mitigation. Use only what is in my notes; mark missing information with [TO ADD] and do not invent achievements, dates or results.
```

## Quality check

Every achievement, output and date in the draft matches your notes, and each risk is one you recognise as real.

## Limits

- Risk: Your own unpublished drafts, reviews or third-party content.

## Key concepts

- **Vitae Researcher Development Framework (RDF)** – A UK framework of researcher competencies in four domains: knowledge and intellectual abilities; personal effectiveness; research governance and organisation; engagement, influence and impact. Use it to evidence training and skills development in reviews.
- **Risk register and likelihood–impact matrix** – Each risk is listed with likelihood and impact (for example scored 1–5), their product as priority, a mitigation and an owner, following the logic of ISO 31000 risk management. Review it at each milestone.
- **SMART objectives** – Doran's (1981) criteria: specific, measurable, assignable or achievable, realistic and time-related. Next year's objectives written this way can be assessed at the following review.
