# Choosing the review type & protocol

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/literatur-7

## Goal

Check whether a systematic, scoping or narrative review fits your question, the literature and your time – and draft the protocol, including testable inclusion and exclusion criteria and borderline cases.

A reasoned choice of review type for your question and deadline, and a protocol outline along PRISMA-P with testable criteria and borderline cases – ready to discuss with your supervisor.

## When to use

It is tempting to call a review 'systematic' without the time or literature to support it; vague inclusion criteria then fail halfway through screening.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste your research question, the expected amount of literature and your deadline.
2. Ask your AI assistant to compare systematic, scoping, narrative and rapid reviews for your case.
3. Ask for a protocol outline with testable inclusion and exclusion criteria and borderline cases.
4. Apply the criteria to five abstracts you know and compare the outline with your field's reporting guideline.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] planning a literature review. My research question: [question]. Expected amount of literature: [few / moderate / large, if known]. Time available: [weeks or months]. Compare a systematic review, a scoping review, a narrative review and a rapid review for my case in a table: purpose, what my question would need, effort, and risks. Recommend one type and name the conditions under which you would recommend another. Then draft a protocol outline: review question, its elements (for example PICOC), sources and databases, time span and languages, inclusion and exclusion criteria, screening stages, data extraction and synthesis approach, and whether registering the protocol (for example on PROSPERO or OSF) is usual in my field. For the inclusion and exclusion criteria, derive them from the research question only, show a table (criterion, include or exclude, judged at title-and-abstract or full-text stage, example of a borderline case) and say which criteria are too open, too strict or hard to judge from an abstract. Mark anything you are unsure about.
```

## Quality check

The recommendation fits your question and your time, the criteria give the decisions you expect for five abstracts you know, and the outline covers the items of your field's reporting guideline (for example PRISMA-P or the JBI guidance for scoping reviews).

## Limits

- Choosing the review type is a methodological decision for you and your supervisors; the assistant's outline is a starting point, not a protocol.
- Risk: Your own unpublished drafts, reviews or third-party content.

## Key concepts

- **Review typology (Grant & Booth)** – Fourteen review types compared by search, appraisal, synthesis and analysis (the SALSA framework). Systematic reviews answer focused questions with exhaustive searches and appraisal; scoping reviews map evidence; narrative reviews are selective.
- **Systematic vs scoping review (Munn et al.)** – Choose a scoping review to identify the types of evidence, clarify concepts, map gaps or prepare a systematic review; choose a systematic review to answer a specific question, for example on effectiveness or meaning.
- **Scoping framework (Arksey & O'Malley)** – Five stages: identify the question, identify relevant studies, select studies, chart the data, collate and report; an optional sixth stage is consultation. Report scoping reviews with PRISMA-ScR.
- **PRISMA-P** – A 17-item checklist for reporting systematic review protocols: rationale, eligibility criteria, sources, search strategy, selection and extraction, risk of bias, synthesis. Registering the protocol on PROSPERO (health-related outcomes) or OSF (any field) fixes the plan in advance.
- **Rapid review** – A streamlined systematic approach – for example fewer databases, or single screening with verification – for decisions under time pressure. Report every shortcut as a limitation.
