# Synthesis & concept matrix

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/literatur-5

## Goal

Compare findings across studies, flag contradictions and draft a concept matrix (Webster & Watson) showing which concepts each study covers fully, partly or not at all.

A concept-centric matrix (Webster & Watson) showing which study covers which concept, with evidence, and a first map of agreements and contradictions – the basis for a synthesis you write yourself.

## When to use

After reading twenty papers you know them one by one, but not how they relate – where they agree, where they contradict each other and why.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste or attach the studies and list the concepts you want compared.
2. Ask for a concept matrix with studies as rows and concepts as columns.
3. Ask where the studies agree, contradict each other, and why that might be.
4. Check every cell for two studies against the originals.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] working on a review of [topic]. Using only the studies I provide, build a concept matrix: one row per study, columns for [concept 1], [concept 2], [concept 3]. In each cell, say whether the study addresses the concept fully, partly or not at all, and give the finding in a few words, including direction of effect, with a short supporting passage. Then list where the studies agree and where they contradict each other, with possible reasons such as method, sample or context, naming the study for each point. Do not add studies I have not given you.

[paste abstracts or attach files]
```

## Quality check

Every cell for two studies matches the originals; be cautious, because the assistant can smooth over contradictions or overstate agreement, so the synthesis and its argument must be your own.

## Limits

- Risk: Personal or sensitive data, or decisions that feed directly into your results.

## Key concepts

- **Concept matrix (Webster & Watson)** – A table with studies as rows and concepts as columns that turns an author-centric list into a concept-centric review. Cells record whether and how each study addresses a concept; empty columns point to possible gaps.
- **Narrative synthesis (Popay et al.)** – Structured textual synthesis when meta-analysis is not possible, in four elements: develop a theory of how, why and for whom the intervention or phenomenon works, develop a preliminary synthesis, explore relationships within and between studies, and assess the robustness of the synthesis – more than summarising paper by paper.
- **SWiM reporting guideline** – Synthesis Without Meta-analysis: nine items for reporting how studies were grouped, which standardised metric and synthesis method were used, and how heterogeneity and certainty of evidence were assessed (Campbell et al., 2020).
- **Vote counting and its limits** – Counting studies 'for' and 'against' ignores effect size, precision and study quality; if used at all, count by direction of effect. Contradictions often trace back to method, sample, measure or context.
- **Human check and AI declaration** – Interpreting agreement and contradiction is your scholarly contribution. Verify every cell you rely on, keep the matrix as an audit trail, and declare AI assistance as your university and target journal require.
