# Research-informed teaching

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/transfer-2

## Goal

Develop case studies, exercises and questions for your teaching from current research findings.

A seminar case, discussion questions at the apply level of Bloom's revised taxonomy or above, and a data exercise aligned to your learning outcome, with simplifications listed for you to check.

## When to use

Bringing current research into seminars means building cases and questions from scratch, and recall questions are quicker to write than ones that make students apply or evaluate findings.

## Procedure

1. Attach the paper and state the module, level and learning outcome.
2. Ask for a short case study based on the paper.
3. Ask for three discussion questions and one applied exercise.
4. Check that the case facts match the paper and the questions need application.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.
- Instructions: case length and format, questions at the apply level of Bloom's revised taxonomy or above, outcome, activity and assessment aligned, facts only from the attached paper, a list of simplifications, worked answers with code. Knowledge: your module descriptor and learning outcomes. Switch on "Create documents, charts and code" for data exercises. Starter prompt: "Seminar case and three questions from this paper". Reusable for every new paper and module.

## Starter prompt

```text
I teach [module] to [level] students in [discipline]. Attached is a recent paper: [title]. The learning outcome is: [paste outcome]. Write a case study of about 400 words based on the paper's setting and findings, suitable for a 20-minute seminar activity. Add three discussion questions that ask students to apply or evaluate the findings, not just recall them, and one short applied exercise. Keep facts and figures as in the paper, and list any simplifications you made so I can check them.
```

## Quality check

Every fact and figure in the case can be found in the paper, and each question asks students to apply, compare or judge rather than repeat.

## Limits

- Risk: Public or your own non-sensitive material; errors are easy to spot.

## Key concepts

- **Constructive alignment** – Biggs' principle that intended learning outcomes, teaching and learning activities, and assessment tasks are designed to match: students learn what the activity makes them do, so the activity must require what the outcome states.
- **Revised Bloom's taxonomy** – Six cognitive process levels – remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate, create (Anderson & Krathwohl 2001). Write discussion questions and exercises at apply level or above when the outcome asks for more than recall.
- **Research–teaching nexus (Healey)** – Four ways to bring research into teaching: research-led (learning findings), research-oriented (learning how research is done), research-tutored (discussing papers), research-based (students do inquiry). A data exercise moves a session towards research-based.
- **Case-based learning** – Students work on a realistic, bounded scenario with a question or decision, applying concepts to evidence. Cases drawn from a real paper should keep its facts and say where they simplify.
