# Interdisciplinary bridges

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/idee-4

## Goal

Suggest concepts and methods from neighbouring disciplines that transfer to your research question.

A table of transferable concepts or methods, what each needs adapting and where the transfer could break – plus the search terms that field uses, so you can read further.

## When to use

Useful concepts and methods often already exist in a neighbouring discipline, but you rarely read its journals and do not know the vocabulary to search for them.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste your research question and name your discipline.
2. Ask for concepts or methods from neighbouring disciplines that could transfer.
3. Ask what would need adapting and where the transfer could fail.
4. Look up one suggested concept to confirm it exists as described.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] working on: [research question]. Suggest four concepts, theories or methods from neighbouring disciplines that could help answer it. Show a table: discipline, concept or method, how it would transfer to my question, what would need adapting, risk of mismatch. Add one key reference per row only if you are confident it exists, with a link where possible. If you are not sure, say so; do not invent sources.
```

## Quality check

Look up one suggested concept and its reference: the source exists and uses the concept in the way the assistant describes.

## Limits

- Risk: Public or your own non-sensitive material; errors are easy to spot.

## Key concepts

- **Concept stretching (Sartori)** – Applying a concept to new cases by loosening its definition until it loses meaning. When you transfer a concept, check that its defining attributes still hold in your setting rather than keeping only the label.
- **Boundary objects** – Concepts or tools flexible enough to be used by several communities yet robust enough to keep a shared identity (Star & Griesemer, 1989). Good transfer candidates – and places where disciplines may mean different things by one word.
- **Literature-based discovery (ABC model)** – Swanson's approach links two literatures that do not cite each other through a shared intermediate concept B. A systematic way to find transferable ideas; every candidate still needs checking in the source field.
- **Multi-, inter- and transdisciplinarity** – Multidisciplinary work places disciplines side by side; interdisciplinary work integrates their concepts and methods; transdisciplinary work also involves non-academic partners. Say which level your transfer aims at.
