# Ethics application & data management plan

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/design-4

## Goal

Structure drafts along your institution’s templates – the study-specific reasoning on consent, risks and anonymisation must be your own.

A skeleton under every heading with reviewers' expectations and the questions you must answer – so your time goes into the study-specific reasoning on consent, risk and anonymisation, which stays yours.

## When to use

Ethics forms and data management plans are long and unfamiliar; you lose time working out what each section expects before you can reason about consent, risk, data protection and reuse.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste the template headings and your short study summary.
2. Ask for a skeleton draft under each heading with guiding questions.
3. Ask it to leave consent, risk and anonymisation reasoning as prompts for you.
4. Compare each section with the official template guidance.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am preparing a [ethics application or data management plan] for a study on [topic]. The template headings are: [headings]. My study in brief: [summary]. For each heading, give: what reviewers usually expect, the questions I must answer myself, and a short skeleton with [placeholders]. Do not write study-specific reasoning on consent, risks or anonymisation; leave clear prompts for me instead. Do not state institutional rules or legal requirements unless you are sure; if you are not sure, say so.
```

## Quality check

Each section matches the official template guidance, no institutional rule or legal claim (for example on UK GDPR) appears that you cannot confirm, and the consent and risk reasoning is left for you to write.

## Limits

- Risk: Your own unpublished drafts, reviews or third-party content.

## Key concepts

- **Core principles of ethics review** – Committees look for valid informed consent (information, capacity, voluntariness, right to withdraw), risk of harm proportionate to benefit, confidentiality, and extra care for vulnerable groups. The study-specific reasoning on each must be your own.
- **DCC checklist for a data management plan** – The Digital Curation Centre groups DMP questions into administrative data, data collection, documentation and metadata, ethics and legal compliance, storage and backup, selection and preservation, data sharing, and responsibilities and resources. Many institutional templates use similar headings.
- **FAIR principles** – Data and metadata should be Findable (persistent identifier, rich metadata), Accessible (retrievable by a standard protocol), Interoperable (shared formats and vocabularies) and Reusable (clear licence, provenance, community standards). FAIR does not mean open.
- **Data minimisation, pseudonymisation and anonymisation** – Under UK GDPR you collect only the personal data you need. Pseudonymised data (identifiers replaced, key kept) remain personal data; only data that can no longer identify anyone are anonymous. State which you will hold, and when.
