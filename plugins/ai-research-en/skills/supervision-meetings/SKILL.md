# Supervision meetings

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/querschnitt-0

## Goal

Prepare agendas and questions for supervision and summarise agreed actions afterwards from your own notes.

A timed agenda with the decisions you need and an action list with owners and deadlines checked against your notes – better-used meetings and a shared written record.

## When to use

Supervision time is short: without an agenda key decisions slip, expectations stay implicit, and agreed actions sit in scattered notes that nobody writes up.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste your notes and ask for a 45-minute meeting agenda.
2. Ask for three decisions you need and questions to ask.
3. After a meeting, paste your own notes and ask for an action list.
4. Check the action list against your notes line by line.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am a postgraduate researcher in [discipline] preparing for a [45-minute] supervision meeting. My notes on progress and open issues are: [paste notes]. Draft an agenda with time slots, the three decisions I need from my supervisors, and specific questions for each agenda item. After the meeting I will paste my notes; then summarise them as a table: action, owner, deadline, and add any open questions. Use only what is in my notes and do not add actions or agreements that are not there.
```

## Quality check

Every action in the summary appears in your notes, and nothing you agreed in the meeting is missing.

## Limits

- The AI assistant does not join or record the meeting; it works only from the notes you write and paste.
- Risk: Your own unpublished drafts, reviews or third-party content.

## Key concepts

- **Lee's approaches to doctoral supervision** – Lee (2008) distinguishes five approaches: functional (project management), enculturation (joining the discipline), critical thinking, emancipation (personal development) and relationship development. Naming the approach an agenda item needs clarifies what you ask your supervisor for.
- **SMART actions** – Doran's (1981) criteria for objectives: specific, measurable, assignable (now often 'achievable'), realistic and time-related. An action with a named owner and a deadline can be checked at the next meeting.
- **Decision and action log** – A running record that separates decisions (what was agreed and why), actions (who does what by when) and open questions. It makes progress traceable for annual reviews and protects both you and your supervisors.

## Key references

- Lee, A. (2008). How are doctoral students supervised? Concepts of doctoral research supervision. Studies in Higher Education, 33(3), 267–281. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075070802049202
- Doran, G. T. (1981). There's a S.M.A.R.T. way to write management's goals and objectives. Management Review, 70(11), 35–36.
