# Reference & claim check

> From the knowledge base “AI in the Research Process” (Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences) – https://mkern311.github.io/ai-research-companion-hhn/#/uc/publikation-8

## Goal

Check that every reference exists and its details are correct – and that each cited source really supports the sentence it is cited for.

A per-reference check against DOIs and publisher records, and a support rating (supports, partly supports, does not) for each citing sentence with quoted evidence – you open every flagged source and decide.

## When to use

Wrong reference details and quotation errors – sources that do not say what they are cited for – slip in easily, especially with AI-suggested sources, and damage credibility.

## Workflow with the user

Guide the user through these steps. Where a step says “ask the assistant”, that is you.

1. Paste the paragraph and its references.
2. With web search switched on (if your tool offers it), ask the assistant to check that each reference exists, with a link or DOI.
3. Ask whether each source supports the sentence it is cited for.
4. Open each flagged source and decide yourself.

## Working rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or DOIs; say when you are not sure.
- Mark clearly what comes from the user’s material and what is your own suggestion.
- Stop and ask when the material is missing or ambiguous.

## Typical request

```text
I am checking the references in a paragraph of my [thesis or paper]. For each reference below: 1) search for it and give a working link or DOI; say 'not found' if you cannot find it, and flag differences in authors, year, title or journal. 2) For each sentence with a citation, say whether the cited source – as far as you can see from its abstract or full text – supports the claim, partly supports it, or does not, and quote the passage you rely on. Do not guess; if you cannot see enough of a source, say so.

Paragraph: [paste]
References: [paste]
```

## Quality check

Each 'found' reference opens the right paper, and for two sentences you read the source yourself to confirm the assistant's judgement.

## Limits

- The assistant searches the open web and may not see paywalled full texts; a connected database (for example OpenAlex, which can check whether references exist, or scite for citation statements) makes this more reliable.
- Risk: Your own unpublished drafts, reviews or third-party content.

## Key concepts

- **Quotation (citation) accuracy** – Whether a cited source actually supports the statement it is cited for. Jergas and Baethge's meta-analysis found quotation errors common in medical journals, a substantial share of them major – the same check applies in every field.
- **Reference fabrication by language models** – Language models can produce plausible but non-existent references or real ones with wrong details. Walters and Wilder found both problems in ChatGPT-generated bibliographies, so every AI-suggested reference needs an existence and metadata check.
- **DOI and Crossref metadata** – A Digital Object Identifier resolves to the publisher's record; Crossref metadata confirm authors, year, title, journal, volume and pages. Resolving the DOI is the first existence check, comparing metadata the second.
- **Citation statement classification** – scite classifies citing statements as supporting, contrasting or mentioning (Nicholson et al. 2021). Use the same logic for your rating: supports, partly supports, does not support – always with the quoted passage.
